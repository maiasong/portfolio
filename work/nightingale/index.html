NIGHTINGALE CARING SOLUTIONS

Allowing charge nurses to make shift assignments quickly and without bias.

Role UX designer, UX researcher
Timeline April 2026 - July 2026
Skills
Usability testing · UX design
> Context

Capital One hosts pro bono volunteer cycles every year, and I was excited to join a team to improve the charge nurse experience for Nightingale Caring Solutions.

I have always been interested in UX in healthcare and wanted to apply my design skills to a cause outside of my usual industry.

Nightingale Caring Solutions is a brand new product that allows charge nurses to create shift assignments in an automated, unbiased way. The client was hoping to get feedback from designers on specific areas of improvement and suggestions on the UX and UI.

> Problem

Currently, it is normal industry wide for charge nurses to create shift assignments manually on pencil and paper. It is a very tedious and manually process in an environment that is already fast paced and stressful. Having a more automated and quick way to make these assignments in a way that is still fair would lighten the burden for charge nurses.

> How might we

How might we give charge nurses a faster way to build shift assignments so they can focus less on paperwork and more on their already demanding day?

 
> Research Findings and Recommendations

I conducted 4 interviews with nurses (3 nurses and 1 charge nurse). Ideally, we would have had two interviews per participant to do discovery on their pain points and experiences creating nurse shift assignments, but due to time constraints, we had to combine these interviews into one.

Finding

The nurse's mental model is to drag a nurse into a room, not add a room to a nurse — but the product only supported the latter.

Recommendation

Allow nurses to drag and drop a nurse directly into a room, matching how they already drag a nurse into a shift.

Finding

Similar button styling across the platform made it hard to tell which actions mattered most, like primary vs. advanced setup.

Recommendation

Adopt a primary/secondary button pattern — a filled, high-contrast button for the main action, and a ghost or text-link style for alternatives.

Finding

Nurses had a general sense of the Workload Score but weren't sure exactly how or where it was being used.

Recommendation

Add a tooltip next to the Workload Score explaining what it means and how it factors into assignments.

Finding

Setting a charge nurse was difficult because the action only appeared on hover, making it easy to miss entirely.

Recommendation

Add an overflow icon to nurses (similar to rooms) with a clearly labeled "Charge nurse" button underneath it.
